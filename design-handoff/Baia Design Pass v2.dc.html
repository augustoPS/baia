Pick the ANSI slot whose meaning matches — 1 alert, 2 ok, 3 warn, 4 info — blend toward background until it stops shouting, hand it to readable. Never write a hex. A theme with a pastel ANSI table then gets pastel chrome; a neon one gets neon chrome, which is correct, because that is what its terminal looks like.

Nothing here reads isDark except the repair chain, which already does. bar, divider and hairline all blend toward foreground, which is the dark end on a light theme — so they darken instead of lifting, and the footer stays a step off its surface either way.
INK.FOCUS · FIVE CANDIDATES · THE ONE OPEN DECISION

Dark Pastel has three registers. The brights — six saturated colours built from 0x55 and 0xFF. The greys — #555555, #BBBBBB, #FFFFFF. And one tint, #B5D5FF, which belongs to no ANSI slot at all. State has already claimed four brights: red alert, yellow warn, blue info, green ok. Cyan and magenta are what is left, and a mix of two brights is a fifth road.

Each candidate is shown as a swatch, on a focused footer with its frame, and filling the icon's occupied bay — because the accent decides all three at once. Every other mock in this document is drawn with A, the value shipping today, so implementing as written changes nothing here.

02

The focused pane — the footer is the frame

A 2pt stroke, inset, around the footer bar only. Nothing is drawn over the terminal surface and nothing about the bar changes except its edge — same fill, same height, same ink for everything but the anchor name.

Why this beats the stripe it replaces, in one number: the stripe was 511pt of line along one edge. The frame is 2 × (511 + 22) = 1066pt, and more importantly it closes — enclosure is the fastest shape the visual system resolves, and a 22pt band is small enough that the eye takes the whole rectangle as one object instead of scanning for a line. It is also the product metaphor at the right scale: one plank around one stall.

Attention — two levels, not one

Three requirements that look contradictory: urgent across four panes, tolerable while it waits, settled once acknowledged. They are only contradictory if attention is one state. Level 2 is unacknowledged and loud. Level 1 is acknowledged and still waiting, and it is quiet.

Why the upper third. A prompt sits at the top of a fresh terminal, not the middle of it — centring the glyph made the mark read as a logo, and dropping it to the third line makes it read as a session that has just started. It also puts the largest empty area at the bottom right, which is where the eye leaves the icon, so the mark exhales rather than filling.

Gap 64 is one stroke weight plus a touch — proportional to the drawing rather than the canvas, which is why it holds at 1024 and at 16 without changing character. Chevron and cursor share a stroke weight and an ink baseline at 483 — the cursor's bottom edge and the chevron's lowest ink land on one line, which is what makes them read as a single unit rather than two marks. Mind the cap: a butt cap on a 45° segment overshoots its endpoint, so the path numbers sit ~20 units above the baseline and the drawn edge is what you align. The whole icon is four rectangles and a polyline — no font in the export.

test the badge first

Ship this and re-test NSApp.dockTile.badgeLabel before doing anything else with it. A placeholder icon under ad-hoc signing is the leading suspect in your own notes. If the badge comes back, it — not the window title — is where the waiting count belongs, and §03's title formats become the fallback.

Settings — two keys in the file you already have

With one treatment per problem, most of what v1 proposed making settable no longer exists to set. What is left is genuinely preference: which accent, and how hard a waiting pane shouts.

~/.config/baia/config.json

BaiaSettings already owns this file — JSON, camelCase, seventeen keys, including every value quoted above (fontSize 11.5, backgroundHex #141414, backgroundOpacity 0.85, transparentTitlebar true). Two more, in the spellings it already uses:

"focusAccent":    "accent",   // accent | bone | ansi5 | ansi6 | midnight — a name, never a hex
"attentionStyle": "loud",     // loud | quiet
Settings.swift
two stored properties, values in defaultSettings
no public init — callers mutate defaultSettings; keep it that way
FocusAccent.swift
two enums shaped like CursorStyle — String, Sendable, Equatable, CaseIterable, bare lowercase cases
unlike CursorStyle these are never sent to ghostty, so the spellings are yours to choose
SettingsDecoder
two cases, each via init(rawValue:) like line 140's cursor read
a bad value falls back to its default independently and lands in invalidKeys; a misspelt key lands in unknownKeys
SettingsStore
add both to defaultFileContents
SettingsStoreTests asserts the file names every key the decoder reads
PaneTheme
focusedAccent resolves through the enum, then readable() as always
one switch, five cases; midnight is ansi[4].blended(ansi[5], 0.5)

A settable colour breaks the rule the whole system rests on: chrome matches the terminal theme and never the reverse. The moment a hex is user-editable, switching ghostty themes leaves the chrome behind and every contrast guarantee in §01 is void. focusAccent selects a derivation; the theme still decides what it resolves to.

The footer height, the 2pt frame, the segment priorities, the marker vocabulary, the tier type scale, the icon geometry. Those are the design; making them options would mean there was no design. Note also what no longer needs a key: with focus drawn as an edge and attention as a fill, the two can never collide, so v1's focusStyle validation rule is gone.
